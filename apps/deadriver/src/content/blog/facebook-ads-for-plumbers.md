Ask this in a contractor forum. You get two answers. Both sound very sure. One says Facebook ads are a waste of money for trades. The other says Facebook ads built his business.

Both are telling the truth about their own case. The ad site is almost never the difference. What they put in the ad is. So is what happened in the ten minutes after someone showed interest.

## Do Facebook ads work for plumbers?

Yes. But not for the burst pipe you are picturing. Someone standing in two inches of water does not open Facebook. They search Google. They call the first plumber who picks up.

Facebook ads interrupt people. They do not catch people who are looking. Nobody on Facebook was hunting for a plumber when your ad showed up. So Facebook is a poor tool for today's emergency. It is a good tool for two other jobs:

- **Planned, bigger jobs.** Water heater swaps, repiping, water softeners, sewer line work. People know they need these. They keep putting them off.
- **Being the name they already know.** When the pipe does burst, they search. The plumber they know gets the call.

Judge a Facebook ad by 2am emergency calls and it will look like a flop. Judge it by booked estimates on planned work. The answer changes.

## How much does a Facebook lead cost for a plumbing business?

A lead is a person who gave you their name and number. The newest industry number is for Home and Home Improvement. One lead costs **$41.26** on Facebook lead ads.

That number comes from [WordStream by LocaliQ's Facebook Ads Benchmarks](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2025). It is a yearly study of more than 1,000 ad campaigns. It was last updated September 2025. Here is the full row for Home and Home Improvement.

| Question | Home & Home Improvement |
|---|---|
| What does one lead cost? | $41.26 |
| What does one click cost? | $2.23 |
| Of the people who see the ad, how many click? (click-through rate) | 1.94% |
| Of the people who click, how many become a lead? (conversion rate) | 5.22% |

Two honest warnings before you plan a budget on that. It is a **category average**, not a plumbing number. You are lumped in with remodelers and furniture stores. And it is a national number, not an El Paso number. Treat it as a starting point, not a forecast.

The same study found Facebook lead ads cost **$27.66** per lead on average. [Google Ads](https://ads.google.com/) cost **$70.11**. A click was **$1.92** on Facebook and **$5.26** on Google. Cheaper leads, but colder leads. That trade is the whole story.

## What does $25 a day actually buy?

At $25 a day you spend about **$750 a month**. At the numbers above, that is about 18 leads.

Here is the math. Redo it with your own numbers, not ours.

- $750 a month divided by $2.23 per click is about **336 clicks**.
- 336 clicks at a 5.22% conversion rate is about **17 to 18 leads**.
- $750 divided by the $41.26 cost per lead is about **18 leads**. Same spot.

We suggest starting around $25 a day. That is a suggestion, not a minimum. It is not a promise of 18 leads. It is the budget where the ad platform gets enough data to learn. It learns who should see your ads. Below that, campaigns tend to stall before they get good.

What those leads are worth depends on two things. Your close rate, and your average job. One plumber closes a quarter of 18 leads on water heater work. Another closes two of them on drain snakes. Those are very different places to be. Run your own numbers.

## Why do Facebook ads fail for so many plumbers?

Usually because nobody followed up fast enough. Not because the targeting was wrong. Targeting means choosing who sees the ad.

A Facebook lead is not a phone call. Somebody tapped a form while scrolling. They were half interested. Then they went back to scrolling. They have not committed to anything. Call them the next afternoon and they forgot they filled it out. Often they already booked someone else.

The other three common failures:

1. **Advertising the emergency.** "24/7 emergency plumbing" is a Google message. On Facebook it reaches thousands of people whose plumbing is fine.
2. **Sending clicks to a homepage.** The ad promised one specific thing. The homepage shows a general contractor site. People leave.
3. **Killing it after two weeks.** The campaign spends its early budget learning. Judging it before it has finished learning is judging the wrong thing.

## Facebook ads or Google Ads for a plumber?

Google if you need booked jobs this week. Facebook if you want a pipeline that does not depend on who is searching today. A pipeline is a steady line of future jobs.

[Google Ads](/services/google-ads) catch demand that already exists. Someone typed "water heater repair El Paso" and is ready to call. That is why a Google lead costs about two and a half times more.

Facebook creates demand you did not have. It is cheaper per lead and slower to turn into a job.

Most plumbers we talk to should be on Google first. Add Facebook once the phone side is handled well. Can you only do one thing this quarter? If your [Google Business Profile](https://www.google.com/business/) is a mess, fix that first. Do it before you spend a dollar on either.
