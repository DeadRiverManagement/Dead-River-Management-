You already pay someone for SEO. Now there is a second invoice on the table with search help written on it, or GEO, or AIO, and a pitch that says the thing you have bought for three years is dead.

Some of that pitch is repackaging. Some of it is not. Here is which is which.

If you are not convinced any of this matters yet, start with [why your business needs to show up in AI search](/marketing-advice/ai-search-for-local-business), then come back. This one assumes you are past that and stuck on a narrower question: is search help a different product from the SEO you already buy, and what should you cancel?

## What is the difference between search help and SEO?

SEO earns a ranked link in a list of results. search help earns a mention inside the answer an AI engine writes instead of that list.

That is a change in what the user sees, not in what the machine reads. Classic search hands back ten links and lets the searcher pick. An AI answer hands back one paragraph naming one or two businesses, and most people stop there.

The Pew Research Center measured what that does to clicks. In its 2025 study of 900 US adults and 68,879 Google searches, [users who saw an AI summary clicked a traditional search result on 8% of visits](https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/22/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/), against 15% when no summary appeared. Only 1% clicked a link inside the summary. And 26% ended their session entirely on a page with an AI summary, against 16% without.

Your ranking can hold steady while the clicks it used to produce quietly disappear.

## What is AI SEO called now: search help, GEO or AIO?

search help, GEO and AIO are three names for the same work: getting your business named inside AI-generated answers. No industry body has settled the naming.

search help is answer engine optimization. GEO is generative engine optimization. AIO usually means AI optimization, though some people use it for AI Overviews specifically. Vendors pick whichever acronym sounds newest that quarter, which is why the term keeps changing and the deliverables do not.

The naming is noise. When someone quotes you for search help, GEO or AIO, ask what they will change on your site and profile this month. If they cannot list five concrete things, the acronym is the product.

## Will search help replace SEO?

search help will not replace SEO, because AI engines pull their answers from pages that classic search already crawls, indexes and ranks. The plumbing is shared.

Google is explicit. Its documentation for AI features states that to be eligible as a supporting link, ["a page must be indexed and eligible to be shown in Google Search with a snippet"](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features). Fall out of the index and you fall out of AI Overviews and AI Mode at the same time.

ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini run their own crawlers and live web lookups, so the mechanics differ. The requirement does not. A page a crawler cannot reach, or one built entirely from images and icons with no sentences, is invisible to all of them.

search help is not a replacement for search visibility. It is a second thing search visibility now has to earn.

## Is SEO worth paying for in 2026?

SEO is worth paying for in 2026, but the deliverable changed. Rankings that generate no clicks are worth less than being named in the answer.

Here is the uncomfortable part for whoever sends your current report. If that report is impressions, average position and a keyword count, with nothing tying any of it to booked jobs, you had a problem before AI showed up. AI just made it visible faster.

Adobe Analytics tracked US retail sites in 2025 and found [traffic from generative AI sources up 1,200%](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2025/03/17/adobe-analytics-traffic-to-us-retail-websites-from-generative-ai-sources-jumps-1200-percent) against July 2024, with a 23% lower bounce rate, though it converted 9% less often than other traffic. That is retail e-commerce, not a roofing company in the Lower Valley, so take the direction and not the numbers.

Ask for this instead: which questions in your trade does an AI engine answer without you in it, and what changed on the site this month to fix that. It is the same work that holds up [local SEO in El Paso](/local-seo-el-paso), which is why cancelling everything is the wrong move.

## What does Google say you have to do to show up in AI Overviews?

Google says there are no extra requirements for AI Overviews or AI Mode, and no special file or markup exists that makes a page eligible.

The exact words on [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), last updated December 2025, are: "There are no additional requirements to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, nor other special optimizations necessary." And: "You don't need to create new machine readable files, AI text files, or markup to appear in these features."

Keep that second sentence near the phone. It is the answer to every pitch that wants $500 a month to add an `llms.txt` file to your root directory. Google's own documentation says the opposite of what that pitch depends on.

What Google does recommend is dull, which is why nobody sells it hard:

- Let crawlers in. Check `robots.txt` is not blocking them.
- Write accessible textual content. Real sentences a machine can read.
- Make sure "structured data matches the visible text on the page".
- Keep the Google Business Profile updated.

For a local service business, that last one is most of the job.

## So what does search help work actually look like?

Real search help work is crawlable text written as direct answers, JSON-LD schema that matches the visible page, and a Business Profile that agrees with both.

In the order we do it:

1. **Rewrite pages as answers.** Every page opens by answering the question a customer would type, in one clear sentence, before any selling starts. That sentence is what an engine can lift.
2. **Add schema that is true.** LocalBusiness, Service and FAQPage JSON-LD, matching the words on the page. Schema that contradicts the page is worse than none.
3. **Fix entity consistency.** Same name, same phone, same service area on the site, the profile and every directory that lists you. Conflicting facts drop trust, and AI answers run on trust.
4. **Fill the profile out properly.** Categories, services, service areas, photos, reviews answered. That is what [Google Business Profile optimization](/google-business-profile-el-paso) covers.
5. **Publish answers, not announcements.** One page per real customer question beats twelve posts about company news.

None of that is a secret file. It is [AI search optimization](/ai-search-optimization-el-paso) done as ordinary, checkable work.

## Can ChatGPT do your SEO for you?

ChatGPT can draft content, cluster keywords and write schema, but it cannot crawl your site, fix your Business Profile, or check its own facts.

We use AI tools every day. They are good at first drafts and at turning a messy page into a clear one. They are bad at knowing what is true about your business.

The failure we see most: an owner asks ChatGPT for schema, pastes it in, and now the code says the business opens Sundays and has a street address when the site says neither. Google's guidance is that structured data must match the visible text. That paste broke the thing it was meant to fix.

The second failure is volume. Forty AI articles nobody searched for do not build authority.

## What should you stop paying for?

Stop paying for `llms.txt` files, AI visibility scores with no published method, keyword reports with no booked jobs attached, and rebranded SEO priced as new.

Three questions worth asking any AI SEO agency before you sign:

- **What are you changing on my site this month?** A list of files and pages, not a strategy phase.
- **How do you measure it?** If the answer is a score they invented, ask what the score is made of. If they cannot say, it is decoration.
- **Is this different from what I already pay for?** If your provider already does crawlability, schema and profile work, you are buying most of search help today. Make them prove it before you buy it twice.

## Common questions

### Is GEO the same as search help?

GEO and search help describe the same work under different labels. GEO says generative engine, search help says answer engine. Both mean getting named inside AI answers.

### Do I need an llms.txt file?

Google states no AI text file is needed to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode, so an `llms.txt` file is not a requirement today.

### How long does search help take to work?

search help results follow indexing speed, not a fixed calendar. Pages must be crawled, indexed and then selected, so expect weeks rather than days.

### Does schema markup help with AI search?

Schema markup helps when it matches the visible page. Google's guidance is explicit that structured data must agree with the text a human reads.

### Which AI engines should a local business care about?

Local businesses should start with Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, which sit on searches you already compete for. ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini come next.

### Is search help worth it for a small local business?

search help is worth it for a small local business when the work also improves ranking, because the same crawlable text, schema and profile feed both.

## The one thing to do this week

Ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity the question a customer would ask in your trade and your city, then write down which businesses each one names.

Do it three times with different wording. If the same two competitors come back every time and you are not among them, you know exactly what you are competing for, and it is not a ranking position.

Then read your own homepage first paragraph out loud. If it does not say what you do, where you do it, and how fast, there is nothing there for an engine to quote.
