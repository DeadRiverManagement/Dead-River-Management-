A customer stands in your driveway and says they'll leave you a review. They mean it. Then they get in the car, and by dinner it's gone.

That's the whole problem. Not that customers dislike you. Asking without a link is the same as not asking.

Search this question and half the results drift toward buying reviews, generating them, or filtering out the unhappy ones. All three cost you the profile. Google publishes what it permits, in plain language. That's what this is built on.

## What is the best way to get more Google reviews?

The best way to get more Google reviews is to ask every customer once, right after the job, with a one-tap link or QR code.

No trick underneath that. Google's help page says the permitted method out loud: "To leave reviews, you can ask customers to visit a Google link or scan a QR code" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en), policy current September 2026).

Three pieces: get the link, ask at the right moment, follow up once. If you'd rather it ran without you, that's our [Google Business Profile management](/google-business-profile-el-paso) work.

## Where do you find your Google review link?

Your Google review link lives in your Business Profile under Read Reviews, then Get more reviews. Copy it once and save it in your phone.

The steps, from [Google's own instructions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/16816815?hl=en):

1. Sign in to your Business Profile at business.google.com.
2. Select **Read Reviews**, then **Get more reviews** using the share icon.
3. Select **Copy** to grab the link.

The link is short, usually starting `g.page/r/` and ending in `/review`. Tapping it opens the star box straight away, on a phone, already signed in. No hunting for your business, no scrolling past competitors. That difference is most of your review rate.

Then put it where it gets used: email signature, invoice footer, receipts, a saved shortcut on your phone.

## How do you make a Google review QR code?

Google generates a review QR code inside your Business Profile, beside the review link. Codes can only be created on a desktop computer.

Same screen as above. On **Get more reviews**, right-click the QR code image and choose "Save image as..." to download it. Google states the desktop-only limit plainly, so don't hunt for it in the app.

The code is for the moment you're standing in front of the customer, which is the moment with the highest chance of anything happening. Print it on a card and hand it over with the invoice. A code someone scans in front of you beats an email they'll open Thursday.

## How should you ask for a Google review?

Asking for a Google review works best in person the moment the job ends, followed by the link in a text within the hour.

The in-person ask persuades. The text delivers. Skip either and the rate collapses. Say it plainly: "If you were happy with that, would you mind leaving a Google review? I'll text you the link now." Then send it before you drive off.

Three rules that keep you inside the policy:

- **Ask everyone, not just the customers you're sure about.** Screening the list first is review gating, and Google prohibits merchants who "Discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively solicit positive reviews from customers" ([Google Maps user contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)).
- **Don't hover.** The same policy says merchants should not "require or pressure users to leave ratings or write reviews while on the premises."
- **Don't script it.** Ask for the review, not the wording, and never for five stars.

What Google does permit is the entire method: merchants may "encourage the posting of content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives."

## Can you pay for Google reviews?

No. Paying for Google reviews, or offering discounts and free work in exchange for them, is prohibited by Google and risks account termination.

Google's wording leaves no room: "Offering incentives, like free or discounted goods or services, in exchange for customers to post reviews, change reviews, or remove negative reviews is considered fake & misleading content and is strictly prohibited" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122?hl=en)).

There's a second layer, and it isn't Google's. In August 2024 the Federal Trade Commission announced a final rule on fake consumer reviews, effective 60 days after publication in the Federal Register. It bans compensation "conditioned on the writing of consumer reviews expressing a particular sentiment," and the FTC said the rule "will allow the agency to seek civil penalties against knowing violators" ([FTC, August 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)). Strange bet to place on a $10 review.

## Can Google detect fake reviews?

Google treats fake reviews as fake engagement, states that they will be removed, and lists penalties running from suspended account privileges through to account termination.

The language is blunt: "Fake engagement is not allowed and will be removed." That covers content not based on a real experience, reviews paid for "directly or in kind," and content posted from multiple accounts at one person's request ([Google Maps user contributed content policy](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114?hl=en)).

The real risk isn't deletion. A suspended profile stops showing in the map pack, and for a local service business that's the phone going quiet.

## How many 5-star reviews does it take to raise your Google rating?

Raising a Google rating from 4.0 to 4.5 on a profile with ten reviews takes ten new five-star reviews, because ratings are a running average.

It's division. Ten reviews averaging 4.0 is 40 rating points. Add ten fives and you have 90 points across 20 reviews, which is 4.5 exactly.

Now the direction nobody plans for. Twenty five-star reviews plus one angry one-star is 101 points across 21 reviews, which shows as 4.8. Getting back to 4.9 takes six more fives. Six, from one bad afternoon.

That's the argument for volume. A profile with 15 reviews swings a tenth of a star on one opinion. A profile with 150 barely moves.

## How do you get to 100 Google reviews?

Reaching 100 Google reviews is a division problem: count the jobs you finish each month, ask every one, and measure how many follow through.

Close 40 jobs a month, ask all 40, and you'll get there. The only variable is your response rate, and you shouldn't guess it from a statistic on somebody's blog. Send your next 50 requests, count what lands, and now you know your own number.

Then keep going past the round number, because recency counts: ten reviews from last quarter reads very differently, to a customer and to an AI answer, than 60 that stop in 2023.

## Do Google reviews help your SEO?

Google states that more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking, and reviews now feed the AI answers that recommend businesses too.

Straight from Google's local ranking guidance: prominence is one of the three factors, and "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking" ([Google Business Profile Help](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)). Replies count too: "Positive reviews and helpful replies can help your business stand out."

The newer half is bigger. When someone asks ChatGPT for a good electrician on the Eastside, the model reads your profile, and reviews are the densest evidence on it. ## How should you respond to a bad review?

A bad review needs a reply within a day: acknowledge the problem, say what you're doing about it, and give a direct contact.

You're not writing to the reviewer. You're writing to the next 200 people who read it while deciding whether to call you. They already know one customer was unhappy. They're checking how you behave when that happens.

Three sentences, no arguing in public. "Sorry, that isn't how we run jobs. I've spoken to the crew. Call me directly and I'll make it right." Reply to the good ones too, one line each.

## Common questions

### How can I get free Google reviews?

Every legitimate method is free. Copy your review link, ask each customer in person, text the link the same day, and reply to every review.

### Is Google review automation against the rules?

Automating the request is fine. Automating the review is not. Send the link automatically after each job, then let the customer write whatever they want.

### Can you get a bad Google review removed?

A bad Google review comes down only when it breaks Google's content policy: spam, hate speech, or a fake experience. Genuine opinions stay published.

### What if a competitor leaves a fake review?

Report the review through your Business Profile as fake engagement, which Google's policy says will be removed, then reply calmly while you wait.

### How often should you ask the same customer?

Ask once per job, never twice for the same job. Repeat customers can be asked again after a genuinely new visit.
