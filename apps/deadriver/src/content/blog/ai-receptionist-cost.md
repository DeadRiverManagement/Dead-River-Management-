An AI receptionist is a computer that answers your phone. If you have shopped for one, the prices look odd. One company says $39 a month. Another says $2,100. Both say they answer your phone.

They are not lying. They sell different things. They also bill in different ways. Here is how the pricing really works. Then you can compare them the same way.

## How much does an AI receptionist cost?

Most AI receptionists cost $39 to $299 a month. Services backed by real people start around $300 a month. The big price on the page is not what matters. What matters is the cost when you go past your plan.

Three well-known companies post prices on their own pages. Here is what they showed in September 2026.

| Provider | Entry price | What that includes | After that |
|---|---|---|---|
| [RingCentral AI Receptionist](https://www.ringcentral.com/pricing/ai-receptionist.html) | $49/mo by itself. $39/mo as a RingEX add-on. | 100 minutes. | $0.50 per minute. Billed in 30-second chunks. |
| [Rosie](https://www.heyrosie.com/pricing) | $49/mo. | 250 minutes. | $149/mo for 1,000 minutes. $299/mo for 2,000. |
| [Smith.ai](https://smith.ai/pricing) | $300/mo. | 30 calls. | $11.50 per call over 30. Plus $1.50 per booked appointment. |

Three different units. Minutes, minutes, and calls. That is the whole reason the prices look so different.

## Why is the price range so wide?

Because companies bill on three different units. Each one hides a different risk.

**Per minute.** Cheapest to start. Hardest to predict. RingCentral's entry plan includes 100 minutes. Say your callers talk for three minutes each. That is about 33 calls. Then the overage starts. Overage means the extra charge once you use up your plan. Here it is $0.50 a minute. A busy month can cost many times the sticker price.

**Per call.** Easy to predict per call. Costly per month. Smith.ai's $300 plan covers 30 calls. That is about one call each working day. Booking the appointment costs another $1.50.

**Flat monthly.** You pay the same for 5 conversations or 500. No overage math. No surprise bill.

Before you compare two quotes, do two things. Work out how many calls you miss in a month. Then work out how long a normal call runs. Without those two numbers, every price on this page means nothing.

## Is a text-back system as good as one that answers the phone out loud?

It depends on one thing. Do your customers text?

A voice receptionist picks up and talks with the caller. That is the right tool if your callers are older. Same if the job is an emergency. Same if people need to hear a human voice before they trust you.

A text-back system does not pick up. It catches the call you already missed. Then it turns that call into a text conversation. That is the right tool if your customers like to text. It also fits if your real problem is not "nobody answers". It is "nobody follows up after voicemail".

## Is there a free AI receptionist?

Not really, not for the long run. Some companies offer a free trial or a small free tier. A tier is one level of a plan. But every AI receptionist that answers lots of real calls charges for it. Voice minutes and AI processing cost the company money.

You do already have a free option. You are using it now. Voicemail. The question is not whether voicemail is free. It is this. Some people hang up without leaving a message. Those jobs go unbooked. Do they cost you more than the monthly fee?

## What is cheaper than RingCentral?

On sticker price, a flat monthly plan is usually cheaper than RingCentral's entry tier. That is true once you pass about 100 minutes of calls.

## Is an AI receptionist worth it?

Yes, if one recovered job pays for more than a year of the subscription. That is true for most home service trades.

Do this math with your own numbers, not ours:

1. What is your average job worth? Call it J.
2. How many calls do you miss in a month? Call it M.
3. What share of those would have booked if someone had replied? Call it R.

Your monthly upside is J times M times R. Compare that to the monthly price. Say one job is worth a few hundred dollars. Then recovering even one call a month clears the fee several times over.

We are not putting industry-average numbers in those slots. That is on purpose. Every missed-call stat you have seen comes from a vendor selling the fix. And your business is not the average anyway.

## How do I choose one?

Answer these four questions in order. The choice usually makes itself.

1. **Voice or text?** If your customers will not text, you need a voice product. The flat text-back plans are not for you.
2. **What is your call volume?** Call volume means how many calls you get. Under 100 minutes a month, per-minute pricing wins. Over that, flat pricing usually wins.
3. **Does it book, or just take a message?** A message moves the work back to you. Booking into your calendar is the part that saves time. Check whether booking costs extra.
4. **Who sets it up?** A cheap tool you never finish setting up costs you more. A slightly pricier one that someone sets up for you is the better deal.
