A lead that waits is usually a lead you lost. The ad did not fail. The follow-up did. Speed to lead means how fast a real person answers or calls back. It is often cheaper to fix than buying more clicks.

## What "speed to lead" means here

Speed to lead is how fast a real person replies. A lead is a person who asked about your work. The clock starts when the form, call, or chat reaches you. It stops when someone who can book the job connects.

An auto-text that says "we got it" is not that reply. It can hold the spot. It cannot book the job.

Count nights and weekends the same as Tuesday at noon. The phone rings after hours. Nobody who can book picks up. That minute still counts. The homeowner does not pause their problem until your office opens.

## Why more ad spend won't fix a slow phone

You paid for that attention. Ad spend is the money you pay for ads. Wait too long, and that person calls the next company. Another ad dollar does not call them back.

A missed call shows up as a "bad lead" in the report. So does a call back an hour later. But those were late replies. The form was real. The job went to whoever answered.

Measure booked jobs, not form fills. A form fill is someone filling out your website form. A full inbox and a quiet calendar usually means the phone was slow. Buy more clicks only after the replies are holding.

## Targets that actually change the calendar

Answer or call back in minutes. Not same day. Same day is a long gap. The next company is already on the way.

After hours, have a line a real person answers. That beats a voicemail box that gets checked at 8am. The job that needed you tonight is often booked by morning.

One owner on a truck needs a backup. That can be a person, a service, or a shared queue. A shared queue is a call list others can work. Hope is not a backup. If you are on a job, someone else has to book the next one.

## A simple weekly scoreboard

Check these four numbers before you raise the budget.

- Missed calls.
- Median callback time. That is the middle callback time, not the average.
- Booked appointments from inbound. Inbound means calls and forms that came to you.
- No-shows. That is a separate problem. Do not mix it with speed.

Missed calls and callback time show if a person got there in minutes. Booked appointments show if that speed became work. No-shows stay on the board so you can see them. Keep them off the speed line. Then they cannot excuse a late reply.

## Fixes that usually pay before new campaigns

Do these first. A campaign is a set of ads you pay for. Fix these before you fund another one.

- Call routing that rings a live person. Call routing is where a call gets sent. It has to ring someone even when the owner is on a job.
- A missed-call text within a minute that offers two bookable times. That text has to lead to a person who can book. Not a note that only says you saw it.
- A calendar link only after a human qualifies the job. Qualifies means checks that the job is a real fit. The link is optional. It does not belong in the first auto-reply.
- Stop judging channels until response time is stable. A channel is where a lead came from, like Google or a referral. A slow phone makes every source look like a bad lead.

Intake is the front desk, in plain language. It is who answers, how fast, and if they can book. Get that stable. Then decide if you still need more traffic.
