To track a Forward Air shipment, enter your PRO number or bill of lading number on the [Forward Air tracking page](https://www.forwardair.com/tracking/). The PRO number is the carrier's own reference and returns the most reliable result.

Freight tracking is not parcel tracking. Your shipment scans when it reaches a terminal event, not continuously, so quiet stretches between scans are how the network normally looks. What matters is knowing which number to use, and recognising the difference between a normal gap and a genuine problem.

## How do you track a Forward Air shipment?

Open the Forward Air tracking tool, enter your PRO number, and read the most recent scan and its terminal location. If you do not have a PRO, a bill of lading number will usually work instead.

The order to try:

1. **PRO number** — the carrier's own tracking number, and the one that always resolves to a single shipment.
2. **Bill of lading number** — your document reference. Works when it was keyed consistently at booking.
3. **Shipper or PO reference** — only if that reference was recorded on the shipment at tender.

Read the terminal on the last scan, not just the status word. "In transit" spans everything from just-collected to sitting on the destination dock, and the location is what tells them apart.

## Where do you find your Forward Air PRO number?

The PRO number is printed on the bill of lading, usually top right, and on the pickup receipt the driver leaves at collection. It is also in your booking confirmation and in your TMS shipment record.

If the freight is inbound and someone else arranged it, you will not have it — the shipper does. Asking them for the PRO is faster than trying every reference number you hold against the tracking tool.

## Can you track Forward Air with a bill of lading number?

Yes, and it is the usual route for consignees who have the paperwork but not the carrier's tracking number. Enter the BOL number in place of the PRO on the same tracking page.

The limitation is that a BOL number is your reference, not the carrier's. If it was entered with a prefix, a leading zero, or a different format at booking than the one on your copy, the lookup returns nothing — which looks alarming and usually means a data mismatch rather than lost freight. When a BOL search comes back empty, get the PRO before escalating.

## What is the difference between Forward Air cargo and LTL tracking?

There is one tracking system, but the service determines how the shipment moves and therefore how the scans read. Airport-to-airport cargo moves on a different rhythm from door-to-door LTL freight, so the expected gap between scans differs.

The practical consequence is what "normal" looks like. On a door-to-door LTL move you expect pickup, origin terminal, one or more linehaul scans, destination terminal, then delivery. On an airport-to-airport move the meaningful events cluster at the terminals themselves, and the middle is quieter. Judge a shipment against the service it was booked on, not against a parcel tracking number you checked last week.

## Why has my Forward Air tracking not updated?

Give it two business days. Freight scans at terminal handoffs, so silence mid-lane is expected. If nothing has scanned for longer than that, or the estimated delivery date has passed, stop refreshing and open a trace.

Before you call, rule out the cheap explanations:

- **A wrong digit in the PRO.** By far the most common cause of a dead lookup.
- **A delivery appointment that was never made.** Limited-access, residential and by-appointment consignees stall silently.
- **Weekend and holiday counting.** LTL transit times are business days.
- **Freight held for a paperwork or payment issue**, particularly on collect shipments.

Then call with the PRO number, pickup date, origin and destination ZIPs, and the piece and weight count, and ask for a formal trace rather than a status read-back. Once a trace is open, the destination terminal is the useful contact — they know whether the freight is physically on their dock.

## How do you contact Forward Air about a shipment?

Have the PRO number ready before you call; without it, any conversation is guesswork. Bring the pickup date, both ZIP codes, the piece count and weight, and the delivery receipt if the shipment shows as delivered and you are disputing it.

If you need proof of delivery for a claim or a chargeback, request the signed delivery receipt specifically. The web status showing "delivered" is not the same document, and it will not carry a claim.
